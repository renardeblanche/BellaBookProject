\begin{description}[style=multiline,leftmargin=12mm,labelwidth=9mm,font=\normalfont\itshape]
\item[$\sim$] is used to connect the two verbs of an aspect pair, e.g.\quad
\ru{писа́ть $\sim$ написа́ть} \emph{write}, the imperfective being written first.
The perfective is given in its alphabetical position, with a reference to the imperfective, e.g.\quad
\ru{написа́ть} \emph{pf. of} \ru{писа́ть}, except when it would immediately precede or follow the corresponding aspect pair; e.g.\quad
\ru{описа́ть} is omitted immediately before \ru{опи́сывать $\sim$ описа́ть} \emph{describe}.
\item[*] denotes an obsolete form, cf. p.~80; no accent is given for these obsolete forms.
\item[$\dagger$] (after an imperfective verb) means that the corresponding perfective form exists but is not given in this book, cf. p.~74; the words \emph{no pf.} mean that the corresponding perfective does not exist or is irrelevant for mathematical purposes.
\item[-] at the end of a form means that the form is not a complete word (though occasionally it may look like one). Usually it is a root (e.g. \ru{вод-} \emph{duc- lead}); but sometimes a prefix (e.g. \ru{воз- (вз-)} \emph{out, up}).
\item[\emph{masc.}] after a noun means that the noun is masculine. The gender of the other nouns in this glossary is clear from their form; see p.~37.
\end{description}
The non-mathematical words in the chapter on pronunciation are not included here.
